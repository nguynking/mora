Mora is a work chat for Vietnamese teams and their AI coworkers. The interface is black, white and grey, so the only color on screen is the bots. v4 gives the bots their own character family, the arch family, and adds the tokens and component specs needed to build Mora on web, macOS, Windows, iOS and Android.

Monochrome interfaceGreys carry every surface. No brand accent.
Bots are the colorEight colors, used by bot characters and nothing else.
The face is the statusEyes change shape for each state, so it reads without motion.

Review of v3

What held up, what needed work

The v3 foundations hold: monochrome surfaces, one signal color, plain Vietnamese voice, approval before a bot acts. Three areas needed work: the bots were a near copy of Grok Bot, a few color pairs failed contrast, and there were no specs to build from beyond the web app.

Principles

Delegate, then glance

Six principles come from Grok Bot's design language; the notes are in docs/reference/GROK_BOT_DESIGN.md. Three more are Mora's own, because Mora puts people and bots in the same room and asks before a bot acts.

Does this help the team delegate, or does it give them one more thing to manage?The design test. If the answer is the second, remove it.
Bots

The arch family

Every bot is a flat silhouette standing on the same baseline, with two arch-shaped eyes taken from the Mora mark. The silhouette and color say who it is. The eyes say what it is doing.

Kept from Grok Bot's principles

  • One simple, flat, colored character per bot. Readable at 20 px in a sidebar.
  • A face reduced to eyes. No mouth or nose, so it stays a character, not a portrait.
  • Controlled variation inside one construction. Distinct individuals, one family.
  • Generated in code. Deterministic, never hand-drawn per bot.
  • The avatar is the status. No separate status badges on the face.
  • Bots are the only color in the interface.

Made Mora's own

  • Silhouettes grow from the Mora arch and all stand on one baseline, like coworkers in a row.
  • Arch eyes: flat at the bottom, round at the top. A small copy of the mark.
  • An expression alphabet. Eyes change shape per state; motion adds to it but is never required.
  • A palette with Vietnamese names (Ớt, Cam, Nghệ, Cốm, Lá, Ngọc, Trời, Sen), equal in lightness.
  • Assigned once, stored with the bot. No two bots in a workspace share a look until there are 40.
  • Status text in Vietnamese next to every face.

Construction

One 40 × 40 unit canvas for every bot. Only the top of the silhouette changes; the base, the face line and the eyes stay fixed. That is what makes them read as one family.

  1. Canvas. 40 × 40 units. Render at any size; the path data never changes.
  2. Body box. x 5 to 35 (30 units wide). The top varies per silhouette, between y 4 and y 11.
  3. Baseline. Every body ends flat at y 35 with 4-unit corners. Bots stand; they never float or tilt.
  4. Face line. Eyes sit on y 22.5 on every silhouette, centered at x 14.5 and 25.5 (11 units apart).
  5. Arch eye. 5 wide × 5.4 tall, round top (radius 2.5), flat bottom with 0.8 corners. Eye color bot-eye #141414.
  6. Nothing else. No mouth, nose, outline, gradient, shadow, text or accessories. Detail belongs to the eyes.

Silhouettes

Six tops, each different enough to tell apart at 20 px: round, flat, pointed, double, narrow and scooped. The round arch is reserved for Mora.

Colors

Eight colors at near-equal OKLCH lightness, so no bot looks more important than another. Each keeps 6.9:1 or more with its dark eyes. The small square is the paused tint. A bot looks the same in light and dark themes; only Mora flips.

Expressions

Seven states. The eye shape carries the state on its own; motion adds emphasis and stops under reduced motion. Status text always sits beside the face in rows, headers and the details panel.

Sizes

Below 24 px the eyes are too small to change shape clearly, so faces stay idle and still; the status text carries the state.

Assigning a look

Pick once, store it, never recompute. The first five bots in a workspace get five different silhouettes, the first eight get eight different colors, and no two share a look until there are 40. The hash is defined over UTF-8 bytes of the NFC-normalized id so web, Swift, Kotlin and C# pick the same first candidate. Names typed with a Vietnamese keyboard on macOS can arrive decomposed (NFD), which is why normalization matters when a name is ever used as a seed.

Contrast

  
Checked pairs, WCAG 2.2. Text needs 4.5:1 (3:1 at 18.66 px bold or 24 px and up). Icons and control boundaries need 3:1.

Purple means working
The details icon turns working purple while a bot in the room replies or runs a task. Purple is used nowhere else: not for links, focus, selection or bots.

Vietnamese

  
Vietnamese stacks two marks on one letter (ệ, ổ, ẫ), so it needs more room above the cap height than English.

  

    
Ổn địnhline-height 1.25: marks clear the line above

    
Ổn định0.95 with overflow hidden: the ̂ and ̉ are cut

  

  

    
Set
  • Body line height 1.5; never under 1.2, even for one-line titles.
  • Don't clip text containers vertically. Let them grow.
  • Sentence case: "Tạo kế hoạch", not "Tạo Kế Hoạch".
  • Tabular numbers for times, amounts and counts.

    
Avoid
  • All caps in Vietnamese; marks collide and it reads as shouting.
  • Letter-spacing on Vietnamese body text.
  • Web fonts without full Vietnamese coverage; missing glyphs fall back mid-word.
  • Stripping diacritics anywhere a person can see it, including push notifications and file names.

Motion
Quiet interface, lively bots

    
The interface moves only to explain a change: something appeared, moved or finished. Personality belongs to the bots. Everything here turns off under reduced motion except the bots' eye changes, which are not motion.

Do
  • Scroll to a new message only if the reader is within 80 px of the bottom; otherwise show the jump button with a count.
  • Update plan cards in place; don't re-insert them.
  • Stop every loop when the app is in the background or the bot scrolls off screen.

    
Reduced motion
  • No loops, hops, bounces, shimmer or slides. Use a 120 ms crossfade.
  • Typing dots sit still at half opacity.
  • Bot eye glyphs still change; that is how the state stays visible.

Whatever the stack

  

    
Shared by every build
Tokens, bot kit, component names, copy and the Vietnamese formats. These are the contract; how each app renders them is up to its code.

    
If desktop wraps the web app
Tauri or Electron: keep the web UI, add the native title bar rules above, native menus, notifications and the shortcuts table. Test with the OS text size at 150%.

    
If mobile is native or cross-platform
SwiftUI, Jetpack Compose or React Native: generate tokens per platform from tokens.json, draw bots from bot-kit.json, and use native navigation, sheets and controls.

  






  

    
Voice and content
Plain, first person, one question at a time

    
Bots call themselves mình, address people by name and never guess anh / chị / em. They restate the request, ask one thing, show evidence and mark estimates as estimates. Keep the English words teams already use (PR, review, deploy). No emoji from bots.

Patterns

  

    
Errors
  • What failed, what did not change, what to do: "Chưa gửi được tin nhắn. Tin vẫn nằm trong ô soạn. Thử lại."
  • Never "Đã có lỗi xảy ra" on its own. Never blame the person.
  • Bots add: "Chưa có thay đổi nào được thực thi."

    
Empty states
  • Say what will appear: "Kế hoạch của phòng sẽ hiện ở đây."
  • Offer the one action that fills it: "Tạo kế hoạch".

    
Notifications
  • Title: room name. Body: who and what. "Mora: Kế hoạch “Sửa menu” đã xong."
  • Only notify for mentions, approvals needed, blocked bots and finished work you approved.

    
Bots introduce themselves
  • "Mình là Báo giá, đồng đội AI của nhóm. Mình soạn báo giá bằng VND."
  • Bots never pretend to be people and always carry the AI label.

  






  

    
Accessibility
WCAG 2.2 AA on every platform

    
These are acceptance criteria. A screen isn't done until it passes them in both themes.

  

  

    
See
  • Text 4.5:1, large text and icons 3:1, input boundaries 3:1 (control-border).
  • State never by color alone: words and a shape too.
  • Works in Windows high contrast and forced colors: bubbles get a border, the selected row an outline.

    
Read
  • lang="vi" on the app and lang="en" on English fragments, so screen readers pronounce Vietnamese correctly.
  • Layout holds at 200% text (Dynamic Type AX sizes, Android 200% font scale, browser zoom). Rows wrap to two lines; nothing clips.

    
Operate
  • Every action by keyboard; focus always visible (2 px ring); logical order: sidebar → header → transcript → composer → details.
  • Targets 44 pt iOS, 48 dp Android, 24 px minimum on the web with 32 preferred.
  • No action depends on hover or on a gesture alone; long-press menus also open from a More button.

    
Announce
  • New messages in a polite live region: "{tên}: {nội dung}". Don't announce your own messages.
  • Bot state changes once: "Mora đang làm", "Kiểm thử cần giúp". Not every animation frame.
  • Bot faces are decorative; the name and status carry the meaning: "Kiểm thử, đồng đội AI, cần giúp".

    
Motion
  • Respect reduced motion on every platform; bots keep their expressions.
  • Nothing flashes more than 3 times a second.

    
Test
  • VoiceOver (macOS, iOS), TalkBack, Narrator and NVDA with a Vietnamese voice.
  • Keyboard only, 200% text, both themes, a 320 px wide window.

Before a screen ships

  

    
Design
  • Only tokens; no raw hex, px or ms in code.
  • Light and dark checked.
  • Empty, loading, error and offline states designed.
  • Compact, medium and large window classes checked.

    
Content and access
  • Vietnamese copy reviewed by a native speaker; no guessed pronouns.
  • Keyboard, screen reader, 200% text and reduced motion checked.
  • Bot states have status text next to the face.

  






  

    
From v3 to v4
What changed

    
The idea, the monochrome surfaces, the voice and the local formats stay. The bots are new, a few colors are corrected, and the system now covers native apps.

  

  

    
Areav3v4

    
      
BotsFive primitive shapes, capsule eyes, motion-only status (Grok Bot's system)The arch family: five silhouettes on one baseline, arch eyes, seven expressions

      
Bot colorsEight, including purple and a red close to the error redEight named, equal-lightness colors; no purple; paused tints

      
Bot identityRecomputed from an id hash, collisions possibleAssigned once without collisions, stored, editable

      
LogoArch with capsule eyesArch with arch eyes; clear space, minimums, app icons per platform

      
Color13 tokens, some failing contrastPrimitives + semantic tokens, control-border, destructive #B42318, state layers, one raised dark surface

      
TypeFour stylesA named scale with line heights, platform mapping and Vietnamese rules

      
LayoutPane widths only, inconsistentSpacing, radius, elevation, window classes, density and touch targets

      
ComponentsBubble, plan card, composer described in proseSpecs and states for 34 components
